Legal Resources

The Spirit of Detroit, badge and all.

This is general information compiled from official Michigan and Detroit-area sources, not legal advice. If you're stopped, stay calm, be polite, and comply with lawful orders -- you can always sort out who's right afterward with a lawyer, the League of Michigan Bicyclists, or in court. Asking "am I being detained?" and "what law do you believe I violated?" is reasonable; arguing the law in the moment is not.

Cheat Sheet: A Cyclist's Rights

  • You have all the rights and duties of a motor vehicle driver -- same road, same rules. MCL 257.657
  • You are not required to ride in a bike lane, on a sidepath, or on the shoulder just because one exists. Michigan's Vehicle Code defines "roadway" -- the part of the street the "keep right" rule applies to -- as excluding bike lanes, parking lanes, shoulders, and sidewalks. MCL 257.55. MDOT's own driver brochure says it plainly: bicyclists "are not required to use a bicycle lane and may leave a bicycle lane to turn left or to avoid hazards."
  • When riding slower than traffic you generally keep right, but you may take the full lane when: passing, turning left, the right edge/bike lane is unsafe or unusable (glass, potholes, debris, a door zone, a parked car, a lane too narrow to share), traffic is turning right but you're going straight, or you're on a one-way street. Any one of those is enough. MCL 257.660a
  • Riding at less than the speed of traffic while lawfully keeping right is not "impeding traffic." MCL 257.676b
  • You may ride two abreast. MCL 257.660b
  • You may ride on a sidewalk or in a crosswalk (unless a local sign or ordinance says otherwise) if you yield to pedestrians and signal audibly before passing them. MCL 257.660c
  • A civil bicycling infraction generally does not go on your driving record with the Secretary of State, except violations involving a controlled substance, a minor in possession of alcohol, or open intoxicants.

What Drivers (and Officers) Are Supposed to Do

  • Pass you with at least 3 feet of clearance to your left -- or a safe distance and speed if 3 feet isn't practicable -- and may cross into a no-passing zone to do it safely. MCL 257.636(2)-(3)
  • It's illegal to drive or park in a marked bicycle lane; a vehicle may only cross it to enter/exit a driveway or to make a turn. Mich Admin Code R 28.1322
  • Must yield to you before turning -- no "right hooks" -- and must exercise due care to avoid colliding with you on the roadway. MCL 257.612; Mich Admin Code R 28.1716
  • Opening a car door into traffic (dooring) is itself prohibited. Mich Admin Code R 28.1498
  • A city, township, or village can adopt its own traffic ordinances, but a local ordinance can't be weaker than or conflict with state law. MCL 257.951, 257.605, 257.606
